# CYBERSECURITY JOURNEY CONSTITUTION

### B.Tech ECE → Cybersecurity Professional

### 1. Our Starting Point

We are beginning this journey from **B.Tech ECE, 1st semester**.

We have approximately four years of undergraduate education ahead of us. Therefore, we will not rush into specialization, certifications, or job titles merely for the sake of progress.

Our objective is not to collect certificates.

Our objective is to become a **technically strong, practical, adaptable cybersecurity professional** who understands systems deeply enough to both attack and defend them.

We will use the time available during our degree to build that foundation systematically.

## 2. Our Primary Career Priority

Our career interests are ranked as follows:

1. **Primary specialization:** RED TEAM / PENTESTING
2. **Secondary specialization:** BLUE TEAM / SOC
3. **Supporting specialization:** CLOUD SECURITY
4. **Supporting specialization:** APPLICATION SECURITY / APPSEC

Therefore:

> **RED TEAM / PENTESTING > BLUE TEAM / SOC > CLOUD SECURITY > APPLICATION SECURITY**

However, this ranking does **not** mean that we will ignore the lower-ranked areas.

We will learn all four because cybersecurity is interconnected.

## 3. Where We Want to Go Deep

Our deepest technical focus will be:

### 🔴 RED TEAM / PENTESTING

We want to understand offensive security beyond simply running tools.

We want to understand:

* How systems are built
* How networks communicate
* How applications work
* How authentication works
* How authorization works
* How vulnerabilities occur
* How attackers discover weaknesses
* How vulnerabilities can be exploited in authorized environments
* How privilege escalation works
* How lateral movement works
* How Active Directory environments work
* How web applications can be attacked
* How APIs can be attacked
* How cloud environments can be attacked
* How defenses affect offensive operations
* How to document and communicate findings professionally

The ultimate mindset we want is:

> **"If I were an attacker, how would I approach this system?"**

But always within **legal, authorized environments**.

Our offensive-security knowledge should become deep enough that we understand not merely *which tool to use*, but **why the technique works underneath the tool**.

## 4. Our Second Deep Focus

### 🔵 BLUE TEAM / SOC

We also want strong defensive capability.

We want to understand:

* Security monitoring
* Windows security
* Linux security
* Authentication logs
* Network logs
* Endpoint telemetry
* SIEM
* Detection engineering
* Incident response
* Digital forensics fundamentals
* Threat hunting
* Threat intelligence
* Malware behavior
* Attack detection
* Incident investigation
* Security operations

The fundamental question is:

> **"If someone attacks this environment, how would I know, investigate it, contain it, and prevent it from happening again?"**

This will complement our Red Team knowledge.

We don't want to be someone who only knows how to attack.

We want to understand **what the attack looks like from the defender's side**.

## 5. Why Red Team + Blue Team?

These two areas reinforce each other.

#### Red Team teaches us:

> How attacks happen.

#### Blue Team teaches us:

> How attacks appear.

Together they teach us:

> **How real attacks work from beginning to end.**

A strong offensive professional should understand defensive controls.

A strong defensive professional should understand attacker behavior.

Therefore, we will deliberately develop both perspectives.

Our long-term mindset should become:

```text
                    SYSTEM
                      │
            ┌─────────┴─────────┐
            │                   │
         ATTACK               DEFEND
            │                   │
        RED TEAM            BLUE TEAM
            │                   │
      "How do I get in?"   "How do I detect it?"
            │                   │
            └─────────┬─────────┘
                      │
              COMPLETE SECURITY
                 UNDERSTANDING
```

Cloud knowledge will also strengthen our Red Team and Blue Team capabilities.

## 7. Application Security — Our Fourth Priority

AppSec will remain an important supporting skill.

We want to understand how modern applications are built and how they become vulnerable.

We will learn:

* HTTP/HTTPS
* HTML/CSS fundamentals
* JavaScript fundamentals
* Backend concepts
* Databases
* SQL
* REST APIs
* Authentication
* Authorization
* Sessions
* Cookies
* JWT
* OAuth
* Web architecture

Then security:

* SQL Injection
* XSS
* CSRF
* SSRF
* Command Injection
* Path Traversal
* File Upload vulnerabilities
* Authentication flaws
* Authorization flaws
* API vulnerabilities

We will learn to think through the complete cycle:

```text

    ↓

Fix vulnerability
    ↓
Detect exploitation
```

This will make us stronger in both Red Team and Blue Team.

## 10. Our Certification Philosophy

Certificates are tools, not the destination.

We will **not** collect certifications simply to make our resume longer.

Our priority is:

```text
Knowledge
   ↓
Hands-on Skill
   ↓
Projects / Labs
   ↓
Experience
   ↓
Certification
```

A certification should validate or structure knowledge that we are actually developing.

Potential certification progression:

**CCNA**

↓

**CompTIA Security+**

↓

#### Offensive Security

**eJPT**

↓

**PNPT / OSCP**

#### Defensive Security

**CySA+**

↓

Relevant SIEM / security certifications

#### Cloud

Relevant AWS/Azure security certifications after developing actual cloud skills.

The exact certifications will be reassessed as our skills and career direction become clearer.

## 11. Our Practical Learning Philosophy

We will not become cybersecurity professionals by watching tutorials indefinitely.

For every major concept, we want to move through:

```text
LEARN
  ↓
UNDERSTAND
  ↓
BUILD
  ↓
BREAK
  ↓
INVESTIGATE
  ↓
FIX
  ↓
DOCUMENT
```

For example:

Instead of merely learning SQL Injection:

```text
Learn SQL
   ↓
Understand SQL Injection
   ↓
Attack vulnerable lab
   ↓
Understand why it worked
   ↓
Fix vulnerable application
   ↓
Understand how defenders detect it
   ↓
Document the entire process
```

This is the standard we want to maintain.

## 14. What "Job Ready" Means to Us

We will not define job readiness as:

> "I have enough certificates."

Instead, we will define it as:

> **"Give me a system and I can understand it, assess it, attack it in an authorized environment, detect attacks against it, investigate what happened, and recommend how to secure it."**

That is the standard.

## 16. The Principles We Will Not Break

#### Principle 1 — Fundamentals before advanced tools

We will understand networking, operating systems and programming before trying to master advanced exploitation.

#### Principle 2 — Understanding before memorization

We will understand *why*, not merely memorize *how*.

#### Principle 3 — Practice before certification

We will build skills before collecting certificates.

#### Principle 4 — Attack and defense together

Our Red Team knowledge should strengthen our Blue Team knowledge, and vice versa.

#### Principle 5 — Legal and authorized practice only

All offensive security practice will remain within:

* Our own systems
* Dedicated labs
* CTFs
* Training platforms
* Explicitly authorized environments

#### Principle 6 — Documentation matters

We will document projects, findings, experiments and lessons learned.

#### Principle 7 — Don't rush specialization

Our current priority is exploration and foundation.

Our specialization can become more precise after we gain real exposure.

#### Principle 8 — Build, don't just consume

Less passive watching.

More:

**Lab → Experiment → Break → Investigate → Fix → Document.**

#### Principle 9 — Stay curious

Cybersecurity changes constantly.

Our ability to learn will matter more than any single technology.

#### Principle 10 — Depth over certificate collection

One deeply understood technology is worth more than ten certificates we cannot practically apply.

## 18. The Final Vision

By the end of our B.Tech, we do not want to merely say:

> "I studied cybersecurity."

We want to be able to say:

> **"I understand how modern systems work, how attackers compromise them, how defenders detect and respond to those attacks, how cloud environments change the security model, and how vulnerable applications can be secured."**

Our **primary battlefield is Red Team / Pentesting**.

Our **second perspective is Blue Team / SOC**.

Cloud Security and Application Security will provide the additional depth necessary to understand modern environments.

And our ECE background remains an additional asset that we can potentially extend into embedded, IoT, hardware, automotive or OT security later.
